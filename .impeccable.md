## Design Context

### Users
Pharmacists and dispensing staff at counters or workstations. They process prescriptions quickly under real-world pressure — speed and legibility matter more than visual flair. They need to scan a result and act, not read carefully.

### Brand Personality
Clinical, precise, trustworthy. Three words: **sharp, calm, reliable**. Not cold — there's a human at the counter — but purposeful and unhurried.

### Aesthetic Direction
Clean & confident. Light mode (bright counter/clinic environment). Generous whitespace within tight information groupings. Strong typographic hierarchy. Cyan primary (195 85% 45%) for interactive elements and trust signals. No dark mode priority, no gradient text, no glassmorphism, no side-stripe borders.

**Font**: Geist (sans) + Geist Mono — precise, neutral, and legible at small sizes without being sterile.

### Design Principles
1. **Scan speed first** — the most important information (drug name, dosage) must be readable in 1–2 seconds per item.
2. **Trust signals, not noise** — verification status (FDA/PNDF) should be visible but never louder than the drug name.
3. **Progressive disclosure** — raw OCR, match method, and secondary data live behind one tap; never shown by default.
4. **Density without clutter** — use a 2-column detail grid within cards, not a tall single-column list.
5. **Prescription as document** — surface patient/doctor/date at the top when present; treat the output as a clinical record, not a UI widget.

### Data Depth
Basics only: name, dosage, quantity, frequency, signa. FDA and PNDF shown as small verified/not-found/pending chips — no full enrichment detail surfaced.
